Before you begin

In the exam, Vietnam is an essay, not a source paper. The big question is "how did a small country beat the USA?", and it is won or lost on argument: taking a clear position and defending it. This lesson builds the opening move of that essay, starting with a skill you already use every day without noticing.

ArgueTake a clear line of argument
EvidenceBack the case with specific facts
WeighJudge it, answer 'to what extent'
About 20 minutes. A gut-check on something familiar, then you build the argument that opens the essay. Your reasoning matters more than your speed.

What this sets up

You just built the opening move of the Vietnam essay: a clear line of argument (this war began anti-colonial and became a Cold War fight), backed with evidence, and weighed to a judgement. That is exactly what earns marks in a discursive essay, and it is exactly what a source-comprehension quiz never teaches.

The same instinct that made you ask "who paid for that banner, and what do they want back?" is the instinct that lets you weigh a historian, a politician or a headline, long after the exam is over.
